## The Season, Honestly

Croatia's boat season runs from roughly mid-May to early October. Within that, the weather is not one thing — it changes character month to month, and so does the coast around it.

Most guides tell you July and August are "the best". They are the busiest. That is not the same claim.

Here is what each part of the season actually feels like from the water.

---

## May — The Coast to Yourself

**Sea temperature:** 17–19°C
**Crowds:** almost none
**Best for:** photographers, walkers, anyone who dislikes queues

May is beautiful and slightly cold. The islands are green in a way they will not be again until next spring — by July the hills have gone gold and stayed there.

You can walk into any restaurant in Komiža without booking. Stiniva is empty. The light is soft and long.

The water, though, is genuinely cool. If your holiday is built around swimming for hours, late May at the earliest.

Wind is the trade-off. Spring is less settled, and a day can turn. We watch the forecast closely in May and stay flexible on route.

---

## June — The One We Would Pick

**Sea temperature:** 21–23°C
**Crowds:** moderate, rising through the month
**Best for:** almost everyone

If you asked us to choose one month, it would be June.

The sea has warmed enough to swim properly. The days are at their longest — light until nearly nine in the evening, which changes what a "full day" means. The Blue Cave light window is at its most reliable because the sun is at its highest.

And the coast has not filled up yet. Early June especially still feels like the shoulder season with summer water.

---

## July and August — Peak, With All That Means

**Sea temperature:** 24–27°C
**Crowds:** heavy
**Best for:** swimming, families tied to school holidays, a certain kind of energy

The water is at its best. It is bath-warm in the shallow lagoons, and you can stay in until you are wrinkled.

Everything else is busier. Hvar town in August is loud and expensive. The Blue Cave queue can pass an hour. Popular bays that hold four boats in June hold twenty.

None of that makes it bad. It makes it a different holiday, and it rewards a different strategy: leave earlier than everyone else, eat lunch in Komiža rather than Hvar, and go to the well-known places at the hours the crowds are not.

We plan August days around avoidance. It works, but it needs planning — book well ahead, and be flexible on start time.

One more thing about August: the **maestral**, the afternoon thermal wind, is at its most dependable. It is pleasant on land and choppy at sea. Afternoon crossings get bumpier.

---

## September — The Local Favourite

**Sea temperature:** 23–25°C
**Crowds:** thinning fast after the first week
**Best for:** couples, food, calm water

Ask anyone who lives here and they will say September.

The sea has spent all summer absorbing heat and is still warm — often warmer than late June. The crowds go home after the first week. The light gets that low gold quality that makes everything photograph well. Restaurants have time for you again.

The days are shorter, which shortens a full-day route slightly. That is the only real cost.

If your dates are flexible and you have no children in school, the first two weeks of September are the best two weeks of the year on this coast.

---

## Early October — The Quiet Edge

**Sea temperature:** 21–22°C
**Crowds:** none
**Best for:** the coast as a place rather than a destination

Still swimmable, still beautiful, and increasingly unsettled. Jugo — the southerly — becomes more frequent, which brings swell and closes the Blue Cave more often.

We run into October, but we plan alternatives.

## Quick Comparison

| | Water | Crowds | Light hours | Blue Cave odds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **May** | Cool | Very low | Long | Moderate |
| **June** | Warm | Moderate | Longest | Best |
| **July–Aug** | Warmest | Heavy | Long | Good, but queues |
| **September** | Warm | Low | Shorter | Good |
| **October** | Cooling | None | Short | Variable |

## What Actually Decides Your Day

Month matters less than the specific morning.

A calm day in May beats a windy day in July, every time. The Adriatic does not care about your booking — a strong jugo will flatten the best-planned itinerary in any month.

Which is why we do two things:

**We watch the forecast from three days out**, and talk to you if it is looking marginal.

**We keep the route flexible.** Sheltered water exists in almost any conditions on this coast — it just may not be where we originally planned to take you.

---

## So, When Should You Come?

- **You want the warmest possible water:** late July to mid-August
- **You want the best all-round day:** June
- **You want warm sea without the crowds:** the first two weeks of September
- **You want the coast to yourself and do not mind cool water:** May
- **You are tied to school holidays:** July or August, and book early

There is no bad month in the season. There are only different versions of the same coast — and knowing which one you are getting is most of what makes a day feel right.
